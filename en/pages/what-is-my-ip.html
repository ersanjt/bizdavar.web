The basics

What is an IP address?

Public IP

The identifier your ISP gives your connection; websites send their responses back to it. Devices behind one router usually share a single public IP.

IPv4 and IPv6

IPv4 is four numbers like 85.15.20.1 and is in short supply. IPv6 is the newer version with vastly more addresses; if your ISP supports it, you will see both.

Private IP

Addresses such as 192.168.x.x only exist inside a home or office network and are not visible from the internet.

When do you need your IP?

  1. Give your public IP to IT support to set up remote access, CCTV cameras or a server.
  2. If a website blocked you by mistake, send them your IP to get unblocked.
  3. Open this page after turning on a VPN to confirm your IP has changed.
  4. Ask your ISP for a static IP for servers, cameras and fixed remote access.
FAQ

IP address questions

What is my IP and how do I find it?

Your public IP appeared at the top as soon as you opened this page. It is the address websites and services see from your connection.

How accurate is the location?

IP location is usually city-level or the ISP hub — not your street address. On mobile data another city may appear.

Can someone find my home address from my IP?

No. An IP only reveals the ISP and an approximate area. Subscriber details stay with the ISP and are released only under a legal order.

Why does my IP keep changing?

Most ISPs assign dynamic IPs that change when the router restarts or as you move on a mobile network.

What is a static IP and do I need one?

A static IP never changes. You need one for servers, cameras, company VPNs and remote access; ISPs offer it for a monthly fee.
