Formulas

How is tank volume calculated?

Vertical cylinder and rectangular

A vertical cylinder holds π × r² × h; a rectangular tank holds length × width × height. For the liquid volume, use the liquid level instead of h.

Horizontal cylinder

In a horizontal tank the liquid cross-section is a circular segment, so volume is not linear with level. Segment area × tank length gives the exact volume.

Spherical tank

A sphere holds (4/3) × π × r³. The liquid volume at level h comes from the spherical cap formula: π × h² × (3r − h) / 3.

Tips for accurate measurement

  1. Measure the inner diameter — wall thickness makes a difference of hundreds of liters on big tanks.
  2. Use the same unit for every dimension; the tool converts the result to liters and m³ for you.
  3. For custody transfer and fuel sales, use a certified strapping (calibration) table.
  4. For continuous monitoring, install a radar or ultrasonic level sensor instead of a dipstick.
FAQ

Tank volume questions

How do I calculate the volume of a cylindrical tank?

Square the radius (half the diameter), multiply by 3.1416, then by the height. With dimensions in meters the result is in m³; multiply by 1000 for liters.

How many liters are in a cubic meter?

One cubic meter is 1000 liters, or about 264.2 US gallons.

Why is a horizontal tank not linear with level?

The liquid surface is widest in the middle and narrowest at the bottom and top. Half the height is 50% of the volume, but 25% of the height is only about 19.6%.

How do I account for dished heads?

This tool assumes flat ends. For 2:1 ellipsoidal heads each head adds roughly π × D³ / 24, which you add to the shell volume.

How can I measure tank level automatically?

Radar level sensors such as VEGAPULS measure the level without touching the liquid and, with a volume table, show the liters in the tank in real time.
