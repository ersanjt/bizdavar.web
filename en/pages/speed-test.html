    What the numbers mean

    Download, upload, ping and jitter explained

    Download speed

    How fast data reaches you, in megabits per second (Mbps). Streaming, browsing and app downloads depend on it.

    Upload speed

    How fast you send data — video calls, cloud backup, sending files and live streaming depend on it.

    Ping (latency)

    Round-trip time to the server in milliseconds. Under 30 ms is excellent, under 60 ms is fine for gaming, above 150 ms feels laggy.

    Jitter

    How much ping varies between packets. Low jitter (under 20 ms) keeps calls and games smooth.

    How to get an accurate result

    1. Pause downloads, streaming and cloud sync on every device before testing.
    2. Test over a cable or next to the router to separate Wi‑Fi problems from line problems.
    3. Turn off VPN and proxy — they route traffic through another country and lower the result.
    4. Run 2–3 tests at different times of day; busy networks are usually slower in the evening.
    5. Compare the result with your plan. Wi‑Fi typically delivers 50–70% of the line speed.
    FAQ

    Internet speed test questions

    How does this speed test work?

    Your browser downloads and uploads test files of increasing size to the nearest Cloudflare data center. Each transfer is timed, the 90th-percentile speed is reported, and ping comes from 20 small requests — the same method as speed.cloudflare.com.

    What is a good internet speed?

    For one person, 25 Mbps download and 5 Mbps upload covers streaming, calls and browsing. A family or home office is comfortable with 100 Mbps or more. For gaming, ping matters more than speed: aim for under 60 ms.

    Why is my result lower than my plan?

    Wi‑Fi interference, distance from the router, other devices, VPNs and peak-hour congestion all reduce speed. Test over a cable to see whether the line itself delivers what you pay for.

    Is the speed test free and safe?

    Yes. It is free, needs no app or account, and only transfers random test data. Your IP address is shown to you but not stored on our servers.

    How much data does a test use?

    The test adapts to your speed: about 15 MB on a slow connection and a few hundred MB on gigabit fibre. On a capped mobile plan, run it over Wi‑Fi.

    What is the difference between Mbps and MB/s?

    Internet speed is measured in megabits per second (Mbps). One megabyte per second (MB/s) equals 8 Mbps, so a 100 Mbps connection downloads at roughly 12.5 MB/s.
