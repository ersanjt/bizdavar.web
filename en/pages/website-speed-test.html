    What the results mean

    What are the speed score and Core Web Vitals?

    Performance score

    A 0–100 score Google builds from several speed metrics. 90+ is green (excellent), 50–89 orange and below 50 red.

    Core Web Vitals

    Google's three official user-experience metrics: LCP (main content loading), INP (response to clicks) and CLS (layout stability). They affect SEO rankings.

    Why speed matters

    Every second of delay lowers conversions, and most mobile users leave a page that takes more than 3 seconds to load.

    How to make your website faster

    1. Convert images to WebP or AVIF, compress them and set the right dimensions.
    2. Lazy-load images and videos below the fold.
    3. Cut extra plugins and third-party scripts (chat widgets, pixels, sliders).
    4. Use browser caching, Brotli/Gzip compression and a CDN such as Cloudflare.
    5. Choose fast hosting with enough CPU and memory, close to your visitors.
    FAQ

    Website speed test questions

    How does this website speed test work?

    Your URL is sent to Google PageSpeed Insights. Google loads the page with Lighthouse and returns the score, metrics and suggestions — the same data Google uses to judge page speed.

    What is a good score?

    90–100 is excellent, 50–89 average and under 50 poor. For Core Web Vitals, aim for LCP under 2.5 s, INP under 200 ms and CLS under 0.1.

    Why is my mobile score lower than desktop?

    The mobile test simulates a slower CPU and network. Because most visits are mobile, Google uses the mobile score for rankings.

    Why does the score change between runs?

    Server load, ads, third-party scripts and the network vary on every run. Run a few tests and look at the average.
